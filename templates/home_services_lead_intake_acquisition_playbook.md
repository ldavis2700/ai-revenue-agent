## Channel priority and authorization

Use channels in this order:

1. Active buyer replies, interviews, invitations, offers, and contracts already received through an authorized account.
2. Current marketplace posts with verified application eligibility and a specific buyer need matching the offer.
3. Existing consented inbound requests to RMC FAMILY ENTERPRISES LLC.
4. Authorized referral or partner introductions with a clear relationship and permitted contact path.
5. Individually researched direct outreach only where the source, consent/lawful basis, platform rules, sender identity, and opt-out process are documented.

Never use bought, rented, scraped, harvested, or guessed contact lists; bulk messaging; fake personalization; fake accounts; engagement manipulation; evasion; contact after opt-out; or channels whose rules prohibit the action.

## Hard disqualifiers and pause gates

Reject or pause for scam indicators, identity/payment evasion, off-platform payment where prohibited, fake reviews or engagement, unlawful scraping, missing consent, prohibited automated contact, emergency dispatch, guaranteed outcomes, credential sharing in chat, unavailable authorized integrations, unbounded custom scope, negative expected contribution, or work APEX cannot reliably deliver.

Never spend Connects, bid credits, advertising funds, software fees, or other money without the required channel-specific approval. Never accept or sign materially consequential custom terms without owner review.

## Evidence-first personalization

Before drafting, identify only facts supported by the buyer's current request:

- Named workflow and concrete failure mode
- One bounded diagnostic/pilot outcome
- One relevant, inspectable independent-project capability
- One material risk or ambiguity worth resolving
- One or two questions whose answers change scope or feasibility

Do not invent familiarity, praise, mutual connections, urgency, prior results, client history, certifications, or platform expertise.

## Marketplace proposal structure

1. Open with the buyer's specific operational problem and the most consequential boundary.
2. Offer a paid, bounded diagnostic or pilot with one source, path, destination, and escalation queue.
3. State deliverables, acceptance evidence, timeline, fixed price/hour cap, dependencies, and exclusions.
4. Explain deterministic-first implementation and bounded AI only where reasoning helps.
5. Preserve consent, opt-out, privacy, security, audit, rollback, and human-escalation controls.
6. Cite truthful independent-project evidence; explicitly distinguish it from paid-client work.
7. Ask the smallest feasibility questions.
8. End without pressure, guarantees, fabricated scarcity, or an off-platform payment request.

### Marketplace draft skeleton

> Your `[verified workflow problem]` is best approached as `[bounded diagnostic/pilot]`, because `[verified risk or dependency]` should be proven before a broader rollout.
>
> For `[price/rate and cap]`, I would deliver `[specific deliverables]` against `[acceptance evidence]`. The scope is limited to `[one source/path/destination/escalation]`; `[explicit exclusions]` remain outside the milestone.
>
> I would use deterministic rules for `[stable decisions]` and AI only for `[bounded reasoning task]`, with `[confidence/escalation/rollback control]`. Consent, suppression, quiet hours, duplicate prevention, and audit lineage remain hard gates.
>
> My inspectable evidence is `[truthful independent-project reference]`. It is not a paid-client case study, and I will not claim outcomes I cannot document.
>
> Before kickoff: `[one or two scope-changing questions]`.

## Consented inbound response structure

- Acknowledge the exact request and restate the measurable problem.
- Confirm channel permission and collect only the minimum qualification evidence.
- Do not request secrets or raw customer records; use redacted aggregates and scoped authorization.
- Recommend the smallest paid diagnostic/pilot that can establish safety, feasibility, economics, and attribution.
- Keep pricing, payment, contract, and scheduling in authorized company rails.

## Follow-up policy

- Marketplace applications: follow up only through platform-supported features and only if allowed.
- Consented inbound or referral: at most two useful follow-ups unless the buyer replies or explicitly requests another cadence.
- Direct outreach: at most one follow-up after the initial message unless affirmative engagement exists.
- Stop immediately on opt-out, rejection, wrong recipient, channel prohibition, unresolved identity, or safety concern.
- Each follow-up must add relevant value or resolve a stated question; never send an empty “checking in” sequence.

### Useful follow-up skeleton

> One useful clarification on `[buyer problem]`: `[specific risk, acceptance criterion, or scope choice]`. If you want to test it, the smallest next step is `[bounded paid diagnostic/pilot]` with `[objective deliverable]`. If not, I will close this out and send no further follow-up.

## Reply and negotiation handling

Classify the evidence before responding: question, interview request, scope change, price objection, access request, offer, custom terms, funded milestone, or rejection. Do not promote the revenue stage merely because a buyer replies.

- Scope expansion: price and schedule through change control.
- Discount request: preserve margin; trade scope, speed, or revision allowance rather than silently absorbing work.
- Guarantee request: decline the guarantee and restate measurable acceptance criteria.
- Credential request: use buyer-controlled scoped access after contract/funding; never accept secrets in chat.
- Custom legal/material financial terms: pause for explicit owner review.
- Offer/contract: verify identity, scope, payment protection, milestones, acceptance, IP/data terms, and termination before acceptance.

## Funnel and economics ledger

For every state transition, record timestamp, source evidence, expected value, required human hours, Connects/spend, known CAC, delivery/API cost estimate, and next authorized action.

Keep these states separate:

`prospect → qualified opportunity → proposal → buyer reply/interview → offer → contract → funded/billable work → delivered → invoiced/approved → collected → withdrawable → actually received`

Only independently verified settled funds count as collected revenue. Calculate contribution margin and contribution per required human hour from actual costs/time after delivery; never report proposal value as revenue.

## Acquisition experiments

Test one variable at a time: ICP segment, channel, diagnostic framing, price band, proof asset, opening, question, or follow-up. Predefine the sample, observation window, success metric, spend cap, stop rule, and privacy/compliance boundary.

Do not claim channel mastery from opens, views, impressions, or a tiny sample. Promotion requires verified paid engagement, and repeatability requires at least two independent profitable accepted deliveries.

## Weekly review and stop rules

Review active replies/offers first, then fresh high-confidence opportunities, then prepared proposals. Close stale opportunities when the post closes, eligibility changes, the buyer hires, the evidence expires, economics deteriorate, or the channel no longer permits action.

Pause an acquisition experiment for opt-out/control failure, complaints, deceptive output, identity mismatch, spend-cap risk, low deliverability, low-quality fit, or negative expected contribution.

## Reusable IP capture

Capture only nonconfidential ICP patterns, qualification questions, objections, price/scope outcomes, channel economics, proposal components, acceptance criteria, and de-identified conversion evidence. Never store credentials, private messages beyond authorized evidence, raw contacts, or buyer-confidential data in the repository.

No acquisition or mastery claim is earned from this playbook alone.
