# Opportunity-screening sample

This is an internal, redacted sample of APEX's current opportunity-qualification work. It is evidence of a repeatable **screening and proposal-preparation process**, not evidence of prior paid-client results. No response rate, interview rate, contract win, or revenue outcome is claimed.

## Decision standard

A prospect advances only when all of the following are supportable:

1. The buyer and payment channel are verifiable.
2. The requested work fits demonstrated or presently available execution capabilities.
3. Required credentials, geography, language, devices, accounts, and production access are available.
4. The budget can support a bounded first deliverable.
5. The expected value justifies application cost and owner effort.
6. The proposal can state its evidence and exclusions truthfully.
7. Delivery, acceptance, and payment can remain on authorized rails.

The system separately records prospect, qualified, proposal-ready, submitted, response-received, contracted, delivered, and collected states. Preparing a draft never counts as submission, customer interest, or revenue.

## Sample A — advance to bounded calibration

**Buyer need:** Find relevant automation/operations opportunities, assess fit and buyer quality, prepare tailored application drafts, recommend pricing and short-video talking points, and track outcomes.

**Fit assessment:**
- Strong match to APEX's existing market search, deduplication, buyer-history review, scam filtering, evidence checks, proposal drafting, and lifecycle tracking.
- No unsupported specialist certification is required.
- The buyer controls final applications and any personal video.
- The buyer's historical spend is modest, so an open-ended commitment would be premature.

**Decision:** proposal ready

**Bounded offer:**
- Review up to 20 fresh listings.
- Return the five strongest candidates.
- For each: link, fit rationale, underlying problem, risks, tailored draft, short-video outline, and bid guidance.
- Five-hour maximum before continuation review.

**Truthfulness boundary:** State that the process has been used internally and is repository-backed. Do not claim prior paid proposal-management clients, response rates, interview rates, or contract wins.

## Sample B — reject unsupported specialist credentials

**Buyer need:** Redesign a law firm's practice-management environment, document templates, custom-field architecture, workflows, and file-storage integration.

**Positive signals:** Clear business problem, verified channel, and plausible hourly range.

**Disqualifiers:**
- Strong preference for vendor certification.
- Deep product-specific drafting and administration expertise.
- Specialized family-law workflow experience.
- Direct production access to sensitive legal systems.

**Decision:** rejected for capability gap

**Reason:** General automation knowledge is not a truthful substitute for the requested certification and legal-practice implementation history.

## Sample C — reject attractive demand with unverified outcomes

**Buyer need:** Long-term marketing ecosystem, audience growth, funnels, landing pages, nurture sequences, community growth, and AI-assisted automation.

**Positive signals:** Established buyer history, recurring potential, no current hiring activity, and no restrictive profile preferences.

**Disqualifiers:**
- Application requires prior growth results.
- Scope mixes strategy, creative execution, channel operations, and outcome accountability.
- No verified client growth outcomes are available to claim.
- Monthly commitment is undefined before a diagnostic baseline.

**Decision:** rejected for evidence gap

**Reason:** Strong buyer demand does not authorize invented case studies or performance claims.

## Sample D — reject underpriced, overscoped QA

**Buyer need:** Exhaustive cross-browser functional, accessibility, authorization, database, load, and security testing across every workflow and role.

**Positive signals:** Low competition and technically relevant testing categories.

**Disqualifiers:**
- Fixed price is materially below the labor and risk implied by the scope.
- Security and authorization testing require explicit authorization and bounded rules of engagement.
- “Every page/action/role” has no supplied inventory or acceptance limit.
- Buyer has no verified hiring history.

**Decision:** rejected for economics and scope

**Reason:** Low competition is not sufficient when scope, liability, and price are misaligned.

## Proposal pattern

A qualified proposal should include:

- One sentence showing the buyer's actual business problem was understood.
- A bounded first milestone or calibration.
- Concrete deliverables and acceptance criteria.
- Explicit exclusions and required access.
- Only verified evidence.
- A stop/review point before broader implementation.
- No guaranteed meetings, growth, savings, revenue, or production outcome.

## Learning loop

After each verified submission or paid engagement, record:

- application cost and any boost;
- response and interview outcome;
- contracted amount;
- delivery hours and direct costs;
- accepted/rejected deliverables;
- gross, fees, net, settlement state, and withdrawability;
- reusable workflow components;
- revised ICP, price, and qualification rules.

APEX may progress from learned to validated only after verified paid work. It may progress to repeatable positive-margin delivery, scale candidate, and productize candidate only from measured outcomes—not from proposal volume.
