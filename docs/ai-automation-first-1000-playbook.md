# AI Automation First-$1K Revenue Playbook

Status: learned / proposal-ready. This document is not evidence of a paid engagement, collected revenue, client results, or guaranteed earnings.

## Objective

Prioritize the shortest legitimate path from qualified demand to collected revenue:

1. Find buyers already seeking AI automation, AI consulting, workflow integration, lead capture, CRM automation, or agent implementation.
2. Prefer opportunities where one contract can generate $1,000+ rather than requiring many low-ticket sales.
3. Sell a bounded outcome, not generic "AI".
4. Keep claims truthful, costs explicit, authorization scoped, and delivery evidence-based.
5. Convert successful implementation work into recurring support and reusable delivery assets.

## Primary offers

### A. AI Systems & Workflow Assessment

Target price: $1,000-$5,000 fixed.

Deliverables:
- current-state workflow/system map;
- ranked AI/automation opportunities;
- tool and architecture recommendations;
- implementation roadmap;
- risk/dependency inventory;
- prioritized quick wins;
- optional prototype or implementation phase.

Best fit:
- businesses already using multiple SaaS tools;
- teams with repetitive operational work;
- clients explicitly asking for AI consultation, integration, automation, or scalability.

### B. AI Customer Capture System

Target setup price: $1,000-$2,500 fixed, then optional recurring support.

Deliverables can include:
- 24/7 inbound lead intake;
- approved FAQ handling;
- qualification and routing;
- calendar booking;
- permission-based follow-up;
- CRM logging;
- human handoff;
- monitoring and reporting;
- documented failure handling and test cases.

Do not imply voice/SMS/WhatsApp or regulated-industry functionality unless the required platform, consent, compliance, and client access are confirmed.

### C. Revenue Automation MVP

Target price: $1,500-$5,000 fixed.

Examples:
- lead discovery + enrichment + CRM routing;
- customer follow-up automation;
- internal operations automation;
- AI-assisted document/research workflows;
- database-connected AI agents;
- reporting and QA automation.

## Opportunity qualification

Prefer opportunities with:
- buyer intent already demonstrated by a posted project/request;
- verified payment or low payment risk;
- payout >= $1,000;
- clear scope;
- execution confidence >= 0.80;
- time to cash <= 30 days when evidence supports it;
- reusable delivery components;
- potential recurring support;
- live submission channel;
- reasonable applicant competition relative to remaining openings.

Deprioritize or reject:
- requests requiring fake experience, fake case studies, fake testimonials, or fabricated metrics;
- platform-rule evasion;
- spam or unsolicited bulk outreach;
- unclear payment rails;
- unsafe credential sharing;
- regulated advice outside supported/legal scope;
- vague projects whose delivery cost cannot be bounded;
- speculative work where payout depends primarily on gambling, trading, or uncertain downstream commissions.

## Proposal strategy

A proposal should:

1. Lead with the client's concrete business outcome.
2. Reflect the actual requested scope.
3. State a clear first milestone.
4. Explain how the work will be tested.
5. Mention only real capabilities and real experience.
6. Avoid invented client names, earnings, certifications, portfolios, or results.
7. Quote a price aligned with the stated budget and delivery risk.
8. Preserve enough margin for QA, documentation, and revisions.
9. Avoid attachment clutter unless a relevant real artifact exists.
10. Avoid Connects boosts unless expected value justifies the additional spend.

## Marketplace spend rule

Treat application credits/Connects as acquisition spend.

For every opportunity:
- record application cost;
- record current balance and timestamp;
- require opportunity-specific authorization before spend;
- compare additional boost cost against the expected value of applying to multiple qualified jobs;
- generally prefer diversified high-quality applications over concentrating most available credits on one auction.

A boost is not proof of higher conversion and must not be treated as required.

## Delivery sequence

1. Confirm scope and access.
2. Document acceptance criteria.
3. Build the smallest valuable end-to-end path.
4. Test happy path, edge cases, permission boundaries, retries, and failure states.
5. Demonstrate results in a reproducible way.
6. Deliver documentation and administrative access where promised.
7. Invoice only against contractually supported work.
8. Record collection only from verified payment evidence.
9. Offer maintenance/optimization only when useful and clearly scoped.

## Recurring revenue conversion

After successful delivery, evaluate:
- monitoring;
- monthly QA;
- minor workflow changes;
- usage/cost optimization;
- reporting;
- new automation modules;
- incident response;
- training/documentation updates.

Recurring support should not be auto-enrolled. It must be explicitly offered and contractually accepted.

## Productization ladder

Service -> repeated implementation -> reusable internal template -> standardized managed service -> software product.

Do not productize from theory alone. Require evidence of repeated demand and successful delivery.

## Daily pursuit loop

1. Discover current opportunities.
2. Normalize and score them using verified evidence.
3. Remove unsafe/unqualified work.
4. Rank by expected value, actionability, competition, freshness, and time-to-cash.
5. Prepare proposals for the strongest matches.
6. Submit only when platform policy and required authorization permit.
7. Track responses and client questions.
8. Prepare implementation plans before interviews where feasible.
9. Convert contracted work into tested delivery.
10. Track invoiced -> collected -> withdrawable -> received separately.

## Success metrics

Track:
- qualified opportunities discovered;
- proposal-ready opportunities;
- submissions;
- Connects/acquisition cost per submission;
- replies;
- interviews;
- offers;
- contracts;
- contracted value;
- delivered value;
- invoiced value;
- collected revenue;
- net revenue after platform/application costs;
- recurring revenue;
- reuse rate of delivery components.

Never report contracted, invoiced, collected, withdrawable, or received revenue interchangeably.
