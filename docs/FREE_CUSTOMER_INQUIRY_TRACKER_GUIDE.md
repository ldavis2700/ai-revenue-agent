# Free customer inquiry tracker: quick-start guide

This **free, blank CSV** is a simple way for a small service business to organize real incoming inquiries and keep owner-reviewed reply work visible. It is not a CRM, automatic messaging system, customer database, sales result, or paid fulfillment.

**[Open the blank inquiry tracker CSV](FREE_CUSTOMER_INQUIRY_TRACKER.csv)**

## Five-minute setup

1. Download the CSV and open a **private local copy** in Excel, Google Sheets or another spreadsheet editor. Do not create a public spreadsheet containing customer details.
2. Give each inquiry a local, non-identifying ID such as `INQ-001`. Enter the date received, inbound channel, broad service type, and question category (quote, availability, inclusion, follow-up or concern).
3. Only mark follow-up consent **Yes** if the actual requester and channel authorize it. **Unknown** is not permission to contact anyone.
4. Draft an answer grounded in your verified services, current schedule and approved prices. Mark "Owner-reviewed Reply" Yes only after a human has reviewed it; mark a reply sent only when that actually occurred.
5. Track the current status, the **next permitted action** and its date, then record outcomes from real replies rather than assumptions.

## Useful status values

`New` · `Needs business facts` · `Draft ready for review` · `Reply sent` · `Awaiting customer` · `Closed` · `Opted out`.

These labels are suggestions, not proof of a reply, sale, or appointment. Update dates and outcomes only from actual activity. Never turn an unanswered inquiry into a presumed booking.

## Privacy and spreadsheet safety

Keep customer names, phone numbers, street addresses, full message transcripts, payment details and other identifying information **out of the public CSV template and public repositories**. If you need personal data for legitimate operations, keep it in a private, access-controlled system with appropriate retention and consent. Never paste secrets or personal data into a public GitHub issue.

When importing untrusted text into spreadsheets, treat leading `=`, `+`, `-` and `@` as potentially executable formulas. Prefer categorical summaries instead of pasting raw customer text. Sharing a blank template does not grant permission for automated SMS/email marketing, contact uploads, or repeated follow-up.
