01 Capture the facts before you quote

Choose one inbox your team will review. For each new inquiry, record a request ID, the time received, service type, general service area, preferred date, and the next action. Keep customer contact details in your existing protected business system.

For a residential clean, ask about bedrooms, bathrooms, and whether the customer needs a standard, deep, or move-out clean. Do not promise a price or time slot before checking your actual scope and calendar.

“Thanks for reaching out. Which type of clean do you need, how many bedrooms and bathrooms are there, and what ZIP code is the home in?”

Practice: Write your three most useful intake questions. Remove any question you do not need to answer the request.

02 Use AI as a draft assistant

Start with approved business facts: services, coverage area, pricing rules, and handoff contact. Give an AI tool only the minimum information it needs. Use fictional or redacted customer details while practicing.

Draft a brief reply for a residential cleaning inquiry.
Use only these approved business facts: [facts].
Customer question, with identifying details removed: [question].
Ask one useful next-step question. Do not invent prices,
availability, discounts, guarantees, or completed bookings.
If information is missing, flag it for the business to review.

Practice: Test a normal inquiry, an out-of-area request, and a complaint. Review each draft before using it. Complaints, sensitive information, and uncertain facts need a person.

03 Follow up without losing trust

Follow up only through an authorized channel for a relevant request. A request for a quote is not blanket permission for a marketing subscription. Check current contact preferences before every message and stop when someone opts out.

“Do you have any questions about the cleaning quote we discussed? If you’d like to continue, reply here and we can review the next step.”

Give each request an owner. Close the loop when the customer books, declines, or asks not to be contacted. A follow-up checklist does not send messages on its own.

Practice: Set your review time and stopping rules. Check duplicate requests before assigning any follow-up.

04 Measure what actually happened

For one week, track inquiry count, first-response time, quotes sent, accepted bookings, opt-outs, and minutes spent handling messages. Use the same definitions the next week.

Compare the median response time and accepted-booking rate. A change in booking rate can come from seasonality, pricing, lead quality, or staff availability; do not automatically credit the templates. Fewer errors and clearer handoffs are useful outcomes too.

Practice: Download the worksheet below. Pick one change to test and record the result, including extra work or complaints.
